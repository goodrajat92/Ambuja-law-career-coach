Before a foreign investor's money can move, a deal lawyer has to answer one question first: **which route does this sector sit on?**

## The two routes

The **Foreign Exchange Management (Non-Debt Instruments) Rules, 2019** ("**NDI Rules**") — notified by the Central Government on 17 October 2019 under FEMA — are the principal rules governing foreign direct investment, foreign portfolio investment, and other non-debt foreign investment into India. Among other things, they fix, sector by sector, which of two entry routes applies:

- **Automatic route** — the investment doesn't need prior approval from the Government of India. The company still has to comply with pricing guidelines and file the prescribed reporting forms with the RBI, but there's no approval gate before the money comes in.
- **Government route** — the investment needs prior approval from the relevant government authority before it can be made.

Most sectors that are open to FDI at all sit on the automatic route by default; a defined list of sensitive or strategically important sectors sits on the government route, or on the automatic route only up to a specified ownership threshold, with government approval required beyond it.

## Not just a binary switch

Two things deal lawyers learn quickly:

1. **Thresholds can split a sector into two routes.** A commonly cited example is banking, where the entry route is automatic up to a defined ownership percentage and shifts to approval beyond it — so "what's the route" can depend on how much you're buying, not just what you're buying into.
2. **The route is only the first gate.** Once you know the route, the NDI Rules (and instruments issued under them) also fix **pricing guidelines** (a floor price for shares issued to a non-resident, generally tied to a fair-value methodology) and any **sectoral caps** on total foreign ownership — both of which shape the term sheet independently of the entry route question.

## Where to check, in practice

Because sectoral caps and routes are amended by notification fairly often, the two working references are:

- the **NDI Rules themselves**, as amended, and
- the **Consolidated FDI Policy** published by DPIIT, which restates the sector-by-sector position in one document and is the everyday reference most practitioners reach for first — while always cross-checking against the underlying NDI Rules notification for the legally binding text.
