If you work anywhere near capital markets, the **SEBI (Issue of Capital and Disclosure Requirements) Regulations, 2018** — universally shortened to **ICDR** — is one of the first rulebooks you'll reach for. SEBI notified it on 11 September 2018, and it came into force on 10 November 2018, repealing the earlier ICDR Regulations, 2009.

## What it actually regulates

The name undersells the scope a little. ICDR governs the conditions and disclosures for several kinds of capital-raising by companies whose securities are listed, or are proposed to be listed, on a recognised Indian stock exchange, including:

- **Initial public offers (IPOs)** — the classic case: an unlisted company offering shares to the public for the first time.
- **Further public offers and rights issues** — capital raises by already-listed companies.
- **Qualified institutions placements (QIPs)** — a fast-track route for listed companies to raise capital from institutional investors.
- **Preferential issues** — allotments to a select set of investors, common in listed-company PE/strategic deals.

## Why it exists

ICDR's stated purpose is investor protection through disclosure: an issuer raising money from the public must disclose enough, accurately enough, for an investor to make an informed decision — and must follow prescribed eligibility conditions, pricing norms, and lock-in requirements depending on the type of issue.

## How it fits with everything else

ICDR does not operate in isolation. A public issue is also shaped by:

- the **Companies Act, 2013** (for the underlying corporate action — the share issue itself), and
- the stock exchanges' own **listing agreements** and SEBI's **LODR Regulations** (once listed, for ongoing disclosure).

Because SEBI amends ICDR often — the version in force carries a "last amended on" date on SEBI's own site — always check that date before relying on a specific clause in a live matter, and cite the amendment you actually checked.
