A GIFT City fund manager isn't registering under SEBI's familiar AIF Regulations — they're registering under a parallel regime IFSCA built specifically for the International Financial Services Centre: the **IFSCA (Fund Management) Regulations, 2022**.

## The two main scheme types

**Venture Capital Schemes** (which include Angel Schemes) are built for investing in **startups and early-stage venture capital undertakings**. They're capped at **fewer than 50 investors**, and are open to **Accredited Investors** or investors committing above a defined minimum ticket size (commonly cited around USD 250,000) — this is a vehicle built for concentrated, sophisticated-investor capital, not a broad retail product.

**Restricted Schemes** (also called Non-Retail Schemes) cover a much wider range of strategies and are classified, much like domestic AIFs, into **Category I, II and III** depending on the kind of investments made — covering things like SME funds, social venture funds, infrastructure funds, ESG funds and special situations funds.

## Why "Green Channel" matters in practice

Ordinarily, a new fund scheme has to wait for regulatory review before it can start taking money in. IFSCA's **Green Channel** changes that calculus for qualifying schemes (Venture Capital Schemes and Restricted Schemes that raise only from Accredited Investors): the scheme can **open for subscription immediately upon filing** its scheme documents with IFSCA, rather than waiting for prior approval. For a fund manager racing to close a first close before a specific date, this is often the single most practically important feature of the whole regime.

## How this connects to the rest of a cross-border structure

A GIFT City fund doesn't exist in isolation — it typically sits alongside the FDI/FEMA questions covered in [FDI Entry Routes](/Ambuja-law-career-coach/learn/fdi-entry-routes) when the fund itself is investing into Indian portfolio companies, and alongside the tax and treaty analysis that usually motivates choosing GIFT City over a traditional offshore jurisdiction in the first place.
