Two contracting parties can agree "arbitration in Singapore" and mean two very different things — and which one they meant can decide, years later, which country's courts get to hear a challenge to the award.

## The distinction itself

- **Seat of arbitration** — the arbitration's **legal home**. It fixes which country's arbitration law governs the arbitral process and, critically, which courts have **supervisory jurisdiction** — the power to hear challenges to the award, to support or restrain the arbitration, and so on.
- **Venue (or "place") of arbitration** — simply **where the hearings physically happen**. An arbitration can be seated in London but hold hearings in Zurich purely for convenience — the venue has no bearing on which courts supervise the process; only the seat does.

## Why India specifically cares about this

In **Bharat Aluminium Co. v. Kaiser Aluminium Technical Service Inc.** ("**BALCO**"), decided in 2012, a Constitution Bench of the Supreme Court held that **Part I of the Arbitration and Conciliation Act, 1996 — the part giving Indian courts supervisory powers — applies only to arbitrations seated in India**. The Court drew on English law's seat/venue distinction to reach this conclusion, and in doing so overruled its own earlier position (from *Bhatia International*) that had let Indian courts intervene in foreign-seated arbitrations too.

The practical consequence: if two parties choose a **foreign seat**, Indian courts generally have **no supervisory role** over that arbitration at all — even if, say, the parties are Indian, the contract is performed in India, or hearings are physically held in India as a matter of venue convenience. Get the seat wrong (or leave it genuinely ambiguous) and you may find yourself litigating in a court system neither side actually wanted to be in.

## The drafting lesson

Because "seat" and "venue" (or "place") can look interchangeable in plain English, arbitration clauses that just say "arbitration in Mumbai" without specifying whether Mumbai is the **seat** invite exactly the kind of dispute this case law exists to resolve. A well-drafted clause states the seat explicitly and separately from any venue convenience language — for example: *"The seat of arbitration shall be Mumbai. Hearings may be held at any location the tribunal considers convenient."* That one sentence removes an entire category of avoidable post-dispute argument.
