If you learned Indian startup tax practice any time before 2024, "angel tax" was one of the first landmines you were taught to watch for. As of the 2024-25 assessment year, that landmine has been defused.

## What angel tax actually was

**Section 56(2)(viib)** of the Income Tax Act, 1961 (introduced in 2012, originally aimed at money-laundering through inflated share premiums) treated the **excess of a share's issue price over its fair market value** as income in the hands of the *issuing company*, when an unlisted company issued shares to a resident investor above fair value. In practice, this became a recurring headache for genuine startups: a company raising at a valuation an assessing officer later disagreed with could face a real tax demand on the "excess" premium, with the underlying valuation dispute itself often being the actual fight.

DPIIT-recognised startups had a partial exemption route, but the provision — and the valuation disputes it generated — remained a live risk for anyone outside that specific carve-out, and even inside it in practice.

## What changed

Through the **Finance (No. 2) Act, 2024**, the government made **Section 56(2)(viib) inapplicable from 1 April 2025** — meaning it no longer applies from **Assessment Year 2025-26** onward. Critically, this abolition is **general**: it isn't limited to DPIIT-recognised startups, and it applies to share issuances to **any investor, resident or non-resident**. In effect, an unlisted Indian company can now issue shares at any premium to any investor without the company itself facing income tax exposure on the premium under this provision.

## What this doesn't change

Two things worth flagging to a client who hears "angel tax is gone" and assumes valuation no longer matters at all:

1. **The abolition is prospective.** Pending assessments or demands for **AY 2023-24 and AY 2024-25** continue under the old rules — this is relief for future fundraising, not a retroactive wipe of existing disputes.
2. **Fair value still matters for other reasons.** A defensible valuation is still needed for Companies Act compliance (pricing of a preferential allotment), for **FEMA pricing guidelines** on shares issued to non-resident investors (see [FDI Entry Routes](/Ambuja-law-career-coach/learn/fdi-entry-routes)), and potentially for other tax provisions entirely separate from Section 56(2)(viib). "No angel tax" is not the same as "no valuation exercise required."
