Open almost any Indian VC term sheet and the headline instrument is the same three letters: **CCPS**. Understanding it means understanding two layers stacked on top of each other — one statutory, one contractual.

## Layer one: it's a preference share

Under **Section 43** of the Companies Act, 2013, a CCPS is preference share capital: it carries a preferential right to a fixed dividend and to repayment of capital ahead of ordinary equity on a winding up (see [Kinds of Share Capital](/Ambuja-law-career-coach/learn/kinds-of-share-capital)). That's the statutory floor every preference share sits on, CCPS included.

## Layer two: the conversion is bolted on by contract

Nothing in Section 43 requires a preference share to convert into equity. That mechanic — **when** it converts, **at what ratio**, and **on what trigger** — is fixed by:

- the terms attached to the specific class of shares (set out in the company's articles or in the resolution creating the class), and
- the **share subscription agreement** and **shareholders' agreement**, which is where the commercially negotiated detail actually lives.

"Compulsorily" convertible means exactly that: conversion isn't at the investor's option, it *will* happen — typically on the earliest of a fixed long-stop date, an IPO, or a defined liquidity event. That compulsory feature is what distinguishes CCPS from **CCDs** (compulsorily convertible debentures, a debt instrument with the same "must convert" logic) and from **optionally convertible** instruments.

## Why CCPS specifically, and not a simpler instrument

Two practical reasons come up constantly in Indian deals:

1. **FEMA pricing and instrument rules.** Under the NDI Rules, instruments that behave like debt with a fixed, guaranteed return to a non-resident investor can be characterised as debt rather than equity for pricing and reporting purposes. A preference share that is compulsorily (not optionally) convertible into equity, without a guaranteed assured return, is generally treated as an equity instrument — which is usually the outcome both sides want for a foreign VC/PE investment.
2. **Preference over ordinary equity.** As a preference share, CCPS still carries the Section 43 preferential rights (dividend, capital repayment priority) until it converts — giving the investor downside protection that plain equity wouldn't.

## What actually gets negotiated

Because conversion itself is compulsory, term sheet negotiation focuses on:

- the **conversion ratio** (often 1:1 at issuance, but adjustable),
- **anti-dilution protection** — how the ratio adjusts if the company later raises at a lower valuation (see [Anti-Dilution Protection](/Ambuja-law-career-coach/learn/anti-dilution-protection)), and
- the **conversion trigger events** and any long-stop conversion date.
