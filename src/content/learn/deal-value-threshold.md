For years, whether a deal needed the Competition Commission of India's (CCI) approval turned on two numbers: the parties' **assets** and **turnover**. That worked well for traditional M&A — but it had an obvious blind spot for the deals everyone actually worried about.

## The gap the old tests missed

A tech acquisition can be transformative — enormous user base, enormous data, enormous strategic value — while the target itself reports modest assets and near-zero revenue (a common shape for an early-stage but influential digital business). Under the old asset/turnover tests alone, such a deal could close with no CCI review at all, regardless of how much market power was actually changing hands.

## What the Competition (Amendment) Act, 2023 added

The 2023 amendment introduced a **Deal Value Threshold (DVT)**: a transaction needs prior CCI approval if its value — including direct, indirect, immediate, and deferred consideration — **exceeds ₹2,000 crore**, provided the target has **"substantial business operations" in India**. This test became effective from **10 September 2024**, and it applies *regardless of* the target's own asset or turnover figures.

Two design details worth knowing:

- **A safe harbour still applies.** Targets below a defined asset/turnover floor remain exempt from the DVT even where the deal's headline value crosses ₹2,000 crore — the point is to catch a specific class of undervalued-but-influential targets, not to sweep in every acquisition of a small company by a large one.
- **"Substantial business operations in India" is itself a defined, fact-specific test**, not just "the target has an Indian subsidiary." Getting this analysis right is now a routine, load-bearing part of deal timeline planning.

## Why the timeline also matters

The same amendment tightened the review clock: the overall combination review period was cut from 210 to 150 days, and CCI must reach a prima facie opinion within 30 days of notification — after which, absent that opinion, the deal is deemed approved. For a deal team, that makes the *DVT applicability question* (does this deal even need notifying?) one of the first things worth resolving, not a footnote.
