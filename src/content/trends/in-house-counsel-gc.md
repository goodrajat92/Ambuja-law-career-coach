Moving in-house is less a step down from firm practice and more a genuinely different job that happens to require the same legal training.

## What actually changes

At a law firm, you generally have one client relationship at a time and deep specialisation in a narrow area. In-house, you have **one client, permanently** (the business you work for) and a much broader remit — a startup's first legal hire is fielding employment questions, vendor contracts, IP assignment, fundraising documents, and compliance questions in the same week, often without a specialist to hand any of it off to.

This is exactly why the **business skills** category in the Learn tab (reading financials, valuation basics) matters more in-house than it typically does at a firm early on: you're expected to understand the commercial context well enough to give advice that accounts for it, not just the legal position in isolation.

## The judgment shift: risk tolerance isn't yours to set

A firm associate's job is usually to flag every risk clearly and let the client decide. An in-house lawyer is frequently *in the room* when that risk tolerance decision gets made — which means learning to give advice that's calibrated to the business's actual risk appetite, not maximally risk-averse by default. This is a genuine skill, not just a personality trait, and it's built by exposure: seeing how senior in-house counsel or a GC frames a risk for a founder or a board, rather than just listing every possible downside.

## What the path actually looks like

There's no single ladder the way partner track is a single ladder. Common patterns:

- **Firm associate → first in-house hire at a growth-stage company** — broad generalist work, high autonomy, often before there's a formal legal team.
- **Firm associate → specialist in-house role at a larger company** (e.g. a dedicated M&A or securities in-house lawyer), which looks more like a narrower version of firm practice but embedded in one business.
- **In-house generalist → General Counsel**, as the company and legal function both grow — at which point the job shifts again, toward managing a team and sitting genuinely close to strategic decisions, often including a seat that touches the board.

## What to build now if this is the goal

Beyond the legal fundamentals: deliberately practise explaining legal risk in business terms (the Client Communication exercises are aimed exactly at this), and start reading a target company's financials and cap table structure even before you're asked to — the [Break Down a Term Sheet's Economic Terms](/Ambuja-law-career-coach/practice/analysis/analyze-term-sheet-terms) exercise is a reasonable place to start that habit.
