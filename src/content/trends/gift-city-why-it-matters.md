For years, "GIFT City" sounded like a real-estate development in Gujarat more than a place a deal lawyer needed to know cold. That's changed.

## What actually happened

The **International Financial Services Centres Authority Act, 2019** created a single, unified regulator — the **IFSCA** — for financial services carried on within India's International Financial Services Centres (IFSCs). The IFSCA was established on **27 April 2020** and is headquartered at GIFT City, Gandhinagar. Before this, financial activity in an IFSC was regulated piecemeal by whichever domestic regulator would otherwise apply (SEBI, RBI, IRDAI) — the IFSCA replaced that patchwork with one authority and one rulebook for the zone.

## Why deal lawyers should care

An IFSC is treated, for many purposes, as a jurisdiction *outside* India even though it's physically within it — which is the whole point. That gives it a few features that show up directly in deal structuring conversations:

- **Foreign-currency transactions as the norm**, not the exception — IFSC entities can generally transact in foreign currency, which matters for structuring cross-border fund flows.
- **A dedicated fund management regime** — AIFs and other investment vehicles can be set up in the IFSC under IFSCA's own regulations, distinct from SEBI's domestic AIF regulations, which is why more India-focused funds now consider a GIFT City vehicle alongside (or instead of) a Mauritius or Singapore one.
- **Tax incentives** aimed specifically at fund managers and IFSC-based entities, which is the practical reason GIFT City keeps coming up in fund-structuring conversations that would previously have defaulted straight to an offshore centre.

## What to actually watch

This is a fast-moving area — the IFSCA issues new regulations and circulars regularly as the ecosystem matures (fund management, banking, insurance, and capital markets each have their own evolving rulebook). For a deal lawyer, the practical takeaway isn't to memorise today's specific incentives — it's to **know GIFT City is now a live alternative worth checking** whenever a cross-border fund or finance structure is being discussed, and then to go check the IFSCA's current regulations for that specific product, rather than assuming last year's briefing note still holds.
