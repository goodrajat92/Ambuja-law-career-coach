modelAnswer:
  - "The founder's real concern isn't the number '2x' in the abstract — it's what happens in a modest exit. If the company sells for, say, 1.5x the post-money valuation, a 2x participating preference could mean the investor takes the first 2x of their investment back, then *also* shares pro-rata in the remaining proceeds — leaving founders and common shareholders with very little, even though the company technically returned real value."
  - "The investor's real concern is downside protection: they're taking real risk on an early-stage company and want to ensure that in a lukewarm exit (not a failure, but not a big win either), they at least get their money back before common shareholders see anything."
  - "A workable compromise: propose a **1x non-participating** liquidation preference instead of 2x participating. Under this structure, on an exit the investor chooses the better of (a) taking back 1x their investment, or (b) converting to common and taking their pro-rata share of the full proceeds — whichever gives them more. This still protects the investor's downside (they never get less than their money back, assuming there's enough to cover it) without stacking a preference on top of a full pro-rata share in every exit scenario."
  - "Illustrative numbers: on a modest exit only slightly above the money invested, non-participating 1x and participating 2x can produce very different outcomes for founders — worth actually running the two scenarios with real numbers from this deal before finalising a position, since the 'right' compromise depends on the specific valuation and investment amount."
  - "If the investor still wants some participation, a fallback middle position is a **capped participating** preference (e.g. participation capped at a defined total return multiple), which limits how much the investor can take in the very best exits while still giving them some upside sharing — a common landing point when neither side will move fully to the other's preferred structure."

## Brief

You act for the founders in a Series A negotiation. The investor's term sheet proposes a **2x participating liquidation preference**. Your client (the founder) has told you: *"This feels like they get paid twice. I don't understand why we'd agree to this."*

## Instructions

Write out how you would handle this point in the negotiation with the investor's counsel — not just a legal explanation for the founder, but the actual negotiating position and proposed compromise you'd put to the other side.

Cover:
1. What you think is really driving each side's position (not just the stated number).
2. A specific alternative structure you'd propose instead of simply asking them to "lower the multiple."
3. How you'd frame the ask to the investor's counsel — this is a negotiation between two parties who both need the deal to close, not an argument to win.
