modelAnswer:
  - "A workable clause runs roughly as follows: 'If any Shareholder (the \"Selling Shareholder\") proposes to Transfer any Shares to any person (a \"Third-Party Purchaser\") pursuant to a bona fide offer which the Selling Shareholder wishes to accept, the Selling Shareholder shall, before completing such Transfer, deliver a written notice (the \"Transfer Notice\") to the Investor stating: (a) the number of Shares proposed to be Transferred; (b) the identity of the Third-Party Purchaser; (c) the price per Share and all other material terms of the proposed Transfer; and (d) an irrevocable offer to sell such Shares to the Investor on the same terms.'"
  - "The response window: 'The Investor may, within 15 (fifteen) Business Days of receipt of the Transfer Notice (the \"ROFR Period\"), elect by written notice to the Selling Shareholder to purchase all (but not part only) of the Offered Shares on the terms set out in the Transfer Notice.'"
  - "The fallback: 'If the Investor does not exercise its right under this Clause within the ROFR Period, the Selling Shareholder may, for a period of 60 (sixty) days thereafter, Transfer the Offered Shares to the Third-Party Purchaser strictly on terms no more favourable to the Third-Party Purchaser than those set out in the Transfer Notice; failing which the Selling Shareholder must re-issue a Transfer Notice and repeat this process before any Transfer.'"
  - "Watch for the interaction points a strong answer should flag even if not fully drafted: this clause should cross-refer to (and not duplicate) any tag-along clause — typically a ROFR and a tag-along are drafted as alternative, sequential mechanisms (ROFR first; if not exercised, tag-along rights of other shareholders kick in before the third-party sale completes), and the definition of \"Transfer\" in the SHA needs to be wide enough to catch indirect transfers (e.g. a change of control of a corporate shareholder) if that's a real risk in this deal."

## Brief

You act for an early-stage investor holding CCPS in a Series A company. The Shareholders' Agreement (SHA) needs a **Right of First Refusal (ROFR)** clause: if any other shareholder wants to sell their shares to a third party, your client should get the first opportunity to buy those shares on the same terms before the third-party sale can proceed.

## Instructions

Draft a ROFR clause for the SHA covering:

1. **Who it protects** — your client (the Series A investor).
2. **Who it binds** — every other shareholder (founders and any other investors).
3. **The trigger** — a shareholder receives a bona fide offer from a third party they wish to accept.
4. **Notice mechanics** — what must be disclosed (identity of the buyer, price, and material terms) and how (a written "Transfer Notice").
5. **The exercise window** — a defined number of days for your client to elect to buy some or all of the offered shares on the same terms.
6. **The fallback** — if your client doesn't exercise (in full), the seller may complete the sale to the third party, but only on terms no more favourable to the buyer than those offered to your client, and only within a defined period after that.

Keep it to one clause (with numbered sub-clauses), written the way it would actually appear in an SHA — not a summary of what a ROFR clause does.
