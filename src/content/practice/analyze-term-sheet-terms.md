  - "Post-money and ownership: Pre-money valuation ₹40 crore + ₹10 crore invested = ₹50 crore post-money. The investor's ₹10 crore therefore buys 10/50 = 20% of the fully diluted post-money cap table."
  - "The ESOP top-up: the term sheet requires a 10% ESOP pool, created *before* the investment (an 'pre-money' top-up), which is standard investor-favourable structuring. Because the pool is carved out of the pre-money cap table, its cost is borne entirely by the existing (pre-round) shareholders — founders and any earlier investors — diluting them further, while the new investor's 20% is calculated *after* the pool already exists. This is one of the most common places founders under-negotiate, because the effect isn't obvious from the headline valuation number alone."
  - "1x non-participating liquidation preference: on any exit, the investor will take the greater of (a) 1x their ₹10 crore investment back, or (b) their as-converted pro-rata share of the total proceeds. This only meaningfully protects the investor in a *low* exit (below roughly ₹50 crore, where 20% pro-rata would be less than the ₹10 crore they put in) — above that threshold, the investor is better off converting and taking their pro-rata share, so the preference doesn't bite at all in a good outcome."
  - "Founder-friendly or investor-friendly flag: a 1x non-participating preference (rather than a participating or higher-multiple structure) is on the more founder-friendly end of typical market practice — worth confirming with the client that this was actually negotiated down from a harsher opening position, since it's a genuinely good outcome for the founders if so."

## Brief

A founder client has sent you a term sheet with these key terms and asked you to explain, in numbers, what they actually mean before the next investor call:

- Pre-money valuation: ₹40 crore
- New investment: ₹10 crore
- A 10% ESOP pool, to be created before the investment closes ("pre-money")
- 1x non-participating liquidation preference on the investor's CCPS
- Broad-based weighted average anti-dilution

## Instructions

Write up an analysis covering:

1. The **post-money valuation** and the **investor's resulting ownership percentage**.
2. Who actually bears the cost of the **pre-money ESOP top-up**, and why that matters.
3. What the **1x non-participating liquidation preference** does and doesn't protect, with an approximate exit value above which it stops mattering.
4. An overall read: is this term sheet, on the whole, more founder-friendly or more investor-friendly than typical market practice, and why?

Show your arithmetic, not just conclusions.
