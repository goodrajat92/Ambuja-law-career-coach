  - "A clean version: 'Any dispute arising out of or in connection with this Agreement, including any question regarding its existence, validity or termination, shall be referred to and finally resolved by arbitration under the Arbitration and Conciliation Act, 1996. The seat of arbitration shall be Mumbai, India. The arbitration shall be conducted by a sole arbitrator appointed by mutual agreement of the parties, and in the absence of agreement within 30 days, in accordance with the Act. The language of the arbitration shall be English. Hearings may be held at any location the arbitral tribunal considers convenient, which need not be the seat.'"
  - "Why 'seat' matters here specifically: naming Mumbai as the seat (not just 'arbitration in Mumbai') fixes Indian courts' supervisory jurisdiction under Part I of the Arbitration and Conciliation Act, 1996 per BALCO v. Kaiser Aluminium — see the related Learn topic. If the parties actually wanted a foreign seat (say, Singapore) with hearings convenient to India, that's a completely different clause with a completely different jurisdictional consequence, which is exactly why leaving 'seat' ambiguous is a real drafting risk, not a stylistic nitpick."
  - "The governing law point: the law governing the *contract itself* (e.g., Indian substantive law) is conceptually separate from the *procedural law of the arbitration* (usually the law of the seat). A careful drafter states both, even if they're the same jurisdiction, so nothing is left to inference."
  - "A common, avoidable mistake this exercise is testing for: writing 'the arbitration shall be held in Mumbai' and nothing else. That sentence alone is genuinely ambiguous as to whether Mumbai is the seat or merely a venue — precisely the dispute BALCO's fact pattern and its predecessor cases were about."

## Brief

You're drafting the dispute resolution clause for a commercial supply agreement between two Indian companies. Both sides have agreed, in principle, to arbitration rather than litigation — but the term sheet just says "arbitration in Mumbai," which isn't a finished clause.

## Instructions

Draft a complete arbitration clause that:

1. States the **seat** of arbitration explicitly (not just a city name with no further specification).
2. Addresses **venue** separately, so it's clear hearings can happen elsewhere for convenience if needed.
3. Specifies the **number of arbitrators** and how they're appointed.
4. States the **language** of the arbitration.
5. Touches on **governing law** — even briefly — since it's a distinct question from the seat.

Write it as it would actually appear in the contract, not as a description of what the clause should contain.
