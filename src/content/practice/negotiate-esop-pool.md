modelAnswer:
  - "The founder's real cost: a 15% ESOP pool created before the investment closes is carved out of the *pre-money* cap table — meaning it dilutes only the existing shareholders (founders and any earlier investors), not the incoming investor. The new investor's post-money percentage is calculated *after* the pool already exists, so the investor never bears any of that dilution — the founders absorb all of it. This is exactly why the size of a pre-money pool is a real economic term, not a housekeeping detail."
  - "The investor's legitimate concern: a VC wants confidence there's enough option capacity to hire the team the business plan actually requires before the next round — an under-sized pool that runs out mid-way through the year forces an awkward, dilutive top-up later, which is a real problem worth solving for. The fight usually isn't 'pool: yes or no,' it's whether 15% is actually justified by this company's specific hiring plan."
  - "A concrete counter: propose sizing the pool to the company's actual 12-18 month hiring plan (e.g., if the plan supports an 8-10% pool based on planned hires and their likely option grants) rather than accepting a round, unexamined figure like 15% by default. Alternatively, propose a smaller pre-money pool (e.g., 8%) with an agreed mechanism to top it up post-closing, sharing that future dilution across all shareholders (including the new investor) rather than loading it entirely pre-money."
  - "Illustrative math: on a pre-money valuation of Rs 40 crore, a 15% pre-money pool costs the founders roughly twice the dilution of an 8% pool for the same post-money investor percentage — this is the number worth actually running with the real deal figures before proposing a counter, not asserting in the abstract."
  - "Framing to the investor's counsel: present the counter as being about *sizing the pool to a real hiring plan*, not about denying the investor's underlying concern — a plan-based number is easier for the other side to agree to than a bare request for 'a smaller number.'"

## Brief

You act for the founders in a Series A negotiation. The investor's term sheet requires a **15% ESOP pool**, created pre-money, before the investment closes. Your client has asked: *"Why does this come out of our side of the cap table? And is 15% even the right number?"*

## Instructions

Write out how you'd handle this in the negotiation with the investor's counsel. Cover:

1. The mechanic your client is actually asking about — who bears the cost of a pre-money pool, and why.
2. What's likely driving the investor's 15% ask.
3. A specific counter-proposal, with the reasoning and rough math behind it — not just "ask for less."
